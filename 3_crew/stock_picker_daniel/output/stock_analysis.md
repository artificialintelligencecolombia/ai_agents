## 2. Selection Criteria

I weighed four criteria, in order of importance for a 12-month, moderate-risk mandate:

1. **Earnings direction and verifiability:** Are the latest results moving up or down, and can the figures be confirmed?
2. **Balance sheet resilience:** Does the company have room to absorb a weak year?
3. **Identifiable catalysts:** Are there quantified, time-bound drivers over the horizon?
4. **Data quality:** How much of the case rests on conflicting or unverified figures?

## 3. Selected Company: Grupo Argos S.A.

**Criteria won on:**

- **Balance sheet (criterion 2):** Net debt of COP 1.1 trillion and net debt/EBITDA of 0.8x at end-H1 2026 (Grupo Argos investor FAQ, undated). This is the only leverage figure I have for any of the three companies, and it is low.
- **Catalysts (criterion 3):** The ACE 1.0 program commits COP 500,000 million of share repurchases over 12 months, approved by the shareholder assembly. A separate buyback offer of up to COP 100,000 million was announced 26 August 2026. The ACE also targets organic EBITDA growth of more than USD 75 million at Argos Latam over 12–24 months.
- **Scale of profitability (criterion 1, partly):** H1 2026 consolidated EBITDA of COP 1.4 trillion on revenue of COP 5.5 trillion, a margin of about 26% (Grupo Argos 2Q26 release, 14 Aug 2026).
- **Data confidence (criterion 4):** Its headline figures come from the company's own filings and IR materials, and its share price is verified to 27 October.

**Where it is weaker:** Q2 2026 net income fell 7.9% year on year (Reuters via GBM, 17 Aug 2026). A conflicting Valora post reports a 161% first-half increase; I could not reconcile these, so I do not treat the earnings trend as settled. Peer comparison and 2027 guidance are not available.

**Why it ranks first despite this:** Its balance sheet and capital return give it the most room to hold up over 12 months, even if operating earnings stay soft. The other two companies have weaker earnings and less verifiable data.

**Key risk:** Earnings deterioration at Cementos Argos, which is the holding's main operating asset. Falling subsidiary EBITDA (down 5% in Q2) reduces the cash available for buybacks and dividends. Buyback execution is also incomplete: only about 39% of the COP 100,000 million offer was allocated (my calculation from the sourced allocation prices). Since the ACE announcement, ordinary shares are reported up 18%, so some re-rating may already be in the price.

## 4. Rejected Companies

### Rank 2: Cementos Argos S.A. (CEMARGOS): lost on earnings momentum

- **Positives:** Q2 2026 revenue rose 2.4% to COP 1.3 trillion. Colombian local cement volumes rose 10.5% and concrete 15.9% (Grupo Argos 2T2026 presentation).
- **Why it lost:** Q2 adjusted EBITDA fell 5% to COP 280,000 million, with the margin down 175 basis points to 21.2%. Adjusted net income fell 37.1% to COP 154,000 million (Aval Casa de Bolsa note). Revenue growth is not translating into profit, and no quantified 2026 or 2027 guidance is available.
- **Valuation context:** Analyst targets range from COP 11,100 to 15,500 (undated, TradingView). The low target sits at the current price, and the high target implies about 38% upside. The number of analysts is not shown.
- **Unverified item:** The Summit Materials status is unconfirmed, so I cannot weigh a potential US transaction.

It is the closest runner-up. A reversal in margins would move it up, but the current evidence points the wrong way.

### Rank 3: Constructora Conconcreto S.A. (CONCONCRET): lost on earnings and data quality

- **Positives:** New awards of COP 41,485 million in H1 2026. The stock trades near the lower part of its 52-week range (COP 380–598), and reported Q2 revenue growth of +11.86% appears in some coverage.
- **Why it lost:** Q1 2026 EBITDA fell to COP 18,607 million from COP 37,984 million a year earlier, a decline of about half, with a derived margin of about 15.1%. Q2 net profit reportedly fell significantly, though the exact figure is not available.
- **Data quality:** The sources disagree on H1 revenue (COP 171,484 million construction-only versus La República's totals) and on Q2 revenue (COP 178,623 million versus COP 215,909 million). I could not establish a clean trend. The research itself rates this as the lowest-confidence record.
- **Verdict:** Not suitable for an investment view on current evidence. The official Q2 2026 filing needs review first.

## 5. Conclusion

Grupo Argos ranks first because it has the lowest leverage among the three, the only quantified capital-return program, and a reasonable level of data confidence. Its main weakness is the decline in subsidiary earnings and the uncertain earnings trend. Cementos Argos has growing revenue but falling profits, and Conconcreto has the weakest earnings and least reliable data.
